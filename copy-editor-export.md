### Captain's Log
**[title]** The Captain's Log

*Day uncounted, no shore on any chart we know*

We have made landfall. The compass spun for hours before
stilling — this place sits on no map I have ever read.

The local accounts I traded for at the last port spoke of
an Observatory above the clouds, where the Keepers once read
the stars and bound their wisdom to leather and ink. They
are gone now, the Keepers. The Library still stands.

You have asked me to wait at the dock. I will wait. But
the wind has already chosen which way it blows, and you are
the only one of us to hear it.

*The door bears three symbols. Only one was placed by
the Keepers themselves — their spiral, twin to the sigil
within. Press it, and the way will open.*

Find the Keepers' writings. Find what awaits beyond the
sigil and the spiral. Then come back to us — or do not.

— Captain Renn

### Brass Mechanism
**[title]** The Brass Mechanism

You lay a hand on the brass instrument. It is warm.

With a low hum, the gears turn — slowly at first, then with
purpose. Around the rim, an inscription wakes into faint light:
a ring of interlocking spirals matching a pattern you have not
yet seen. Beneath your feet, a great stone slab grinds open.

*A way down has revealed itself.*

### Lectern Book
**[title]** The Lectern Book

The pages are weathered and inked in a careful hand. Diagrams
of brass mechanisms cover the margins. At the center of the
spread, a single sentence:

*"The bookshelves yield their secrets only to those who read.
A passage awaits the patient reader."*

Beneath it, in different ink, a second hand has added:

*"And beyond the passage — three pages, three Ages, and a
way that is not a page at all."*

As you finish the lines, you hear a faint click somewhere
in the room — the sound of wood shifting against wood.

*(close label)* click to close

### Spent Sigil-Plate
**[title]** A Brass Sigil-Plate

A circle of brass, dulled with age. The pearl at its
center is long gone, its empty hollow filling slowly with
library dust.

You kneel and lay a hand on it. The metal is cold — not
the cold of dead stone, but the cold of something that was
warm once, and has remembered to be still.

*Whoever made it is no longer here.*

### The Lighthouse
**[title]** The Lighthouse

The tower is pitch-black, blacker than the water it watches.
Its beam burns steady, but it points *down* — into the
dark — not outward, not warning anyone away.

You wonder what it expects to find in the depths.

*(close label)* click to close

### The Smaller Moon
**[title]** The Smaller Moon

It hangs low above the horizon, paler than its larger
twin. Its craters are arranged in a pattern you almost
recognize — like a face turned just slightly away.

When you look at it directly, it seems to wane.
When you look away, it seems to grow.

*(close label)* click to close

### A Purple Shell
**[title]** A Purple Shell

You crouch and lift one from the wet stones. It is a
deep purple, almost black in the starlight — and warm in
your palm, though everything else on this shore is cold.

When you set it back down, you cannot remember picking
it up.

*(close label)* click to close

### Walk Into the Tide
**[title]** The Tide

You step into the water. It does not retreat from you;
it does not push you back. It pulls you in — gently,
steadily — as if you were always meant to belong to it.

Ahead, the monolith waits beneath the sister moon — black
against her pale light. The water carries you toward it,
though your feet do not move.

The shore recedes. The smaller moon recedes. Somewhere,
a third page is turning.

*The Age accepts you.*

### The Ancient Trees
**[title]** The Ancient Trees

Their trunks rise like the columns of a hall without ceiling —
taller than any mountain you have known, their roots curling above
the earth like the fingers of sleepers. The bark is patient and warm.

Standing before them, you understand that these trees were here
before the Keepers, and will be here when whoever follows you has gone.

*(close label)* click to close

### The Root-Basin
**[title]** The Root-Basin

The roots have woven themselves into a great circular cradle,
and the cradle holds a pool of still water. The water reflects
nothing above you — no leaves, no gold sky, only an overcast
twilight you cannot see.

You look up. Then back down. The reflection does not change.

*(close label)* click to close

### A Small Shell *(green country)*
**[title]** A Small Shell

Resting on the curl of a root, deep purple and warm to the
touch — a shell that should not be here. You have held one like
it before, on a shore beneath twin moons.

Someone has carried it across the worlds. You wonder which
way they were going.

*(close label)* click to close

### Step Between the Roots
**[title]** Between the Roots

You step into the gap. The bark you touched before is now
around you on every side — older than the Keepers, older,
perhaps, than whatever followed them.

The moss closes behind you. The forest does not protest.

The green country holds you the way a cup holds rain — gently,
briefly, without weight.

*The Age accepts you.*

### Left Journal (careful hand)
**[title]** A Journal in a Careful Hand

The page is pristine — kept so carefully it might be a textbook.
Every line measured, every margin even. They wrote about the work:
the brass, the books, the sigils that wanted careful drawing. They
wrote about the sea outside their window. They wrote about her.

*They were not afraid when they wrote this.*

*(close label)* click to close

### Right Journal (different hand)
**[title]** A Journal in a Different Hand

More sketch than text — pages crowded with drawings, half-
glimpses, fragments. Visions and memories. The shape of light at
dawn. Words appear only where the drawings cannot say it.

*The last entry stops mid-thought.*

*(close label)* click to close

### The Keepers' Spiral
**[title]** The Keepers' Spiral

Carved into the stone, deep enough to last. The shape your
hand already remembers — from the dock door, from the cover
of a red book in the chamber above.

This was their mark, and this was their home.

*(close label)* click to close

### A Brass Compass
**[title]** A Brass Compass

Pocket-sized, brass-bezeled, cool in your palm. The needle
has been removed — or fell out, or was never there. The
cardinal points remain, and they no longer mean anything.

You remember the compass on the captain's table, the one
that stopped spinning the day you arrived. You wonder which
of them stopped first.

*(close label)* click to close

### A Small Shell *(cottage)*
**[title]** A Small Shell

Tucked beneath the desk, half-hidden in the shadow — deep purple
and warm to the touch. You have held one like it before — on a shore
beneath twin moons, on a moss-covered root in a green country.

This one is the third. Or perhaps the first. They carried it
with them; or it followed them; or it was always here, and you
have been chasing its likeness across the worlds.

*They did not stay. But they did not go alone.*

### The Loft Door
**[title]** The Loft Door

The door at the top of the stairs has been waiting for you.
The handle is brass and familiar. You climb.

The hum of the wind beyond the stone fades, then sharpens,
then fades again — as if there is more than one wind on the
other side.

*The Age accepts you.*
